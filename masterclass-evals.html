Free Masterclass · Thursday 17 September · 60 minutes

It passed every test he could think of. The room broke it in five minutes.

No slide deck. Dejan builds a live AI product, tries it, and it behaves. Then the whole room hits it from their phones and the log fills with things nobody predicted. They judge the real traces together, discover they do not agree, and write one eval from a failure that was actually there. Sales takes the close.

TLDR

A product gets built from one sentence, on screen, from nothing. Dejan tries it and it behaves. That is the trap: it passed the test of the person who built it. Then the room scans a QR and hits it all at once, and within minutes the log is full of inputs he would never have typed.

They open the log together. Nobody knows what is in it, including Dejan. Everyone judges real traces from their phone, pass or fail. The results come back split: the same output, half the room says fine, half says that should never have shipped. That disagreement is the whole lesson. If forty people cannot agree, a model has no chance, which is why the criterion has to be written down. One eval goes in, gets pushed live, and holds.

Promise of the hour: you stop guessing what your AI gets wrong, you go and look, and you leave having written one eval from a failure you did not predict.

Shape of the hour

01

The story

Everyone tests their own AI feature by typing a few things they can imagine. Poll: have you shipped AI that said something you would be embarrassed by? Yes, no, or we never looked. The third answer usually wins, and that is the hour.

02

Build it from one sentence

A money coach. Tell it what is true right now, it gives you one concrete move you can make this week. Same method as always: the hypothesis is the product, the code is the test. Built live, on the share.

03

It behaves. That is the problem.

Dejan tries the things he would think of. It handles them. It passes his test. Nothing visibly breaks, and that is exactly why he ships it, and exactly why this is different from watching someone poke a product until it falls over.

04

Phones out. Break it.

QR on the live product. The whole room hits it at once with their own money situations. Real inputs, real outputs, piling up in a log none of us has seen.

05

Open the log

First honest look. Investment questions it should have refused. Someone in genuine trouble. A confidently wrong number. Things nobody in the room would have thought to test for, because you cannot imagine your way to other people.

06

Everyone judges · Level 10

Traces go out to phones. Pass or fail, tap through a few each. Not a demo of judging, they actually do it, on outputs their own room produced two minutes ago.

07

The room disagrees · Level 10

Results ranked by how evenly the room split. Top of the board is one output where it is roughly half and half. Nobody planned which one. Same words, one group says helpful, the other says that is advice we cannot give. That is why you write the criterion down.

08

One eval, then theirs

Take a failure everyone agreed on, write the eval, push it, prove it now refuses. Then they do the same on their own. Two people share what they found and what they wrote. Then he stops. Sales does the close.

What they leave with

  • One eval they wrote themselves, from a real failure, live in a product
  • Proof that testing your own thing does not scale, felt rather than told
  • The difference between rules you imagine and failures you find
  • Why two sensible people disagree about the same output
  • A reason to look at traces on Monday instead of guessing

What this is not

  • Not a talk about AI risk or safety
  • Not slides explaining why evals matter
  • Not a walkthrough of a finished product
  • Not a coding class
  • Not Dejan pre-picking the failure to make a point. He does not know what is in the log either.
  • Dejan does not do the sales pitch

Hands-on, almost bulletproof

  • A phone is enough for every hands-on moment: using the product, judging traces, voting. Nothing to install, no account, nothing to redeem beforehand.
  • Small room, thin log: Dejan seeds it with traces from the previous session so there is always enough to judge.
  • If the room happens to agree on everything, he has one trace held back that reliably splits a room. The lesson never depends on luck.
  • Nobody has to share a screen or a link for the hour to work.

Sales bridge

  • You found a real failure and wrote a real eval, in an hour, on a product that was not yours.
  • The wall you hit at the end, the room disagreeing, is exactly what the course solves: how to write a criterion that holds, and how to prove a judge agrees with you.
  • Not “AI is risky, be careful.” You already found the risk yourself. This is how you do it at scale.
